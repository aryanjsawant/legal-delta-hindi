Abstract
Legal Artificial Intelligence (LegalAI) has achieved notable
advances in automating judicial decision-making with the
support of Large Language Models (LLMs). However, ex-
isting legal LLMs still struggle to generate reliable and
interpretable reasoning processes. They often default to
fast-thinking behavior by producing direct answers with-
out explicit multi-step reasoning, limiting their effective-
ness in complex legal scenarios that demand rigorous justi-
fication. To address this challenge, we propose Legal∆, a
reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance le-
gal reasoning through chain-of-thought guided information
gain. During training, Legal ∆employs a dual-mode input
setup—comprising direct answer and reasoning-augmented
modes—and maximizes the information gain between them.
This encourages the model to acquire meaningful reasoning
patterns rather than generating superficial or redundant ex-
planations. Legal ∆follows a two-stage approach: (1) distill-
ing latent reasoning capabilities from a powerful Large Rea-
soning Model (LRM), DeepSeek-R1, and (2) refining rea-
soning quality via differential comparisons, combined with
a multidimensional reward mechanism that assesses both
structural coherence and legal-domain specificity. Experi-
mental results on multiple legal reasoning tasks demon-
strate that Legal ∆outperforms strong baselines in both ac-
curacy and interpretability. It consistently produces more ro-
bust and trustworthy legal judgments without relying on la-
beled preference data. All code and data are available at
https://github.com/NEUIR/LegalDelta.
1 Introduction
The rapid development of LegalAI systems—referring to
artificial intelligence technologies applied to legal practice
and judicial processes—is fundamentally reshaping the le-
gal landscape. By automating complex tasks such as legal
information extraction (Rabelo et al. 2022), intelligent case
retrieval (Zeng et al. 2005; Zhang and Dou 2023), judg-
ment prediction (Liu et al. 2023b), and legal question an-
swering (Louis, van Dijck, and Spanakis 2024), LegalAI sig-
nificantly enhances the efficiency and accessibility of legal
services. These technologies have demonstrated substantial

legal dispute resolution, and regulatory compliance. Given

Recently, researchers have increasingly adopted Rein-

through trial-and-error. Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)
like QwQ (Team 2025) and DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al. 2025)

to the legal domain by distilling reasoning traces from pow-
erful RL-trained models such as DeepSeek-V3 (Liu et al.
2024), as in LawGPT (Zhou et al. 2025). However, these
methods typically rely on rule-based rewards in Reinforce-
ment Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) (Mroueh
2025), which focus on output correctness while neglecting
the quality and depth of the reasoning process itself. There-
fore, how to effectively optimize Chain-of-Thought (COT)
within the RL paradigm remains an open question.
To address these challenges, this paper presents Legal ∆,
a reinforcement learning framework designed to improve le-
gal reasoning in LLMs by incorporating Chain-of-Thought
guided Information Gain . Built upon the GRPO paradigm,
Legal ∆introduces an information-gain based reward mech-
anism into the RLVR setting, encouraging the model to ex-
plore diverse reasoning trajectories and develop robust rea-
soning strategies. Specifically, Legal ∆quantifies informa-
tion gain by leveraging the mathematical equivalence be-
tween model logits and Q-values in reinforcement learning.
By comparing model responses under direct inference and
chain-of-thought prompting, it computes confidence differ-
entials that capture both (i) pointwise mutual information
between reasoning steps and final answer correctness, and
(ii) global confidence shifts indicating whether reasoning
improves decision reliability. This reward is then used to
guide policy optimization, pushing the model to discover
and reinforce high-quality reasoning behaviors that balance
accuracy and prediction confidence.
Our experiments on various LLMs demonstrate that
Legal ∆outperforms all baseline models, achieving approx-
imately 10% performance improvements across multiple
legal reasoning tasks, while also exhibiting strong gener-
alization to out-of-domain tasks. Further analysis reveals
that the information gain module significantly enhances
model confidence levels through progressive logits improve-
ments, while also reducing training batch variance for im-
proved training efficiency and stability. Additionally, com-
prehensive performance quality assessment demonstrates
that Legal ∆increases the prominence of legally tokens
and generates reasoning chains with lower perplexity scores
at the token-level, while consistently outperforming base-
line models across different scenarios complexities at the
sequence-level.
2 Related Work
Existing legal LLMs mainly rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning
(SFT) to help models better understand legal semantics.
These methods usually train the models on legal corpora us-
ing language modeling objectives. For example, models like
LawGPT-zh (Liu et al. 2023a), HanFei (He et al. 2023), and
Lawyer LLaMA (Huang et al. 2023) adopt SFT and com-
bine it with the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) strategy to
improve training efficiency (Hu et al. 2022; Cui et al. 2023;
Deng et al. 2023). One major challenge of SFT lies in the
construction of high-quality training data. Recent work (Yue
et al. 2023) addresses this by collecting existing publicly
available legal datasets, crawling legal texts from the web,
and incorporating instruction-tuning datasets (Zhang et al.2023; Huang et al. 2023; Dai et al. 2023). While these ap-
proaches improve performance to some extent, they typi-
cally require extensive labeled data and focus on end-to-
end optimization over task-specific labels. As a result, SFT-
based methods are often susceptible to overfitting and catas-
trophic forgetting (Luo et al. 2023), which undermines the
retention of previously acquired legal knowledge.
To address this challenge, recent studies (Verma et al.
2025; Vara et al. 2025) have explored Reinforcement Learn-
ing (RL) to encourage LLMs to engage in broader explo-
ration by specially designed reward mechanisms. Intelli-
gent Legal Assistant (Yao et al. 2025) utilizes the cover-
age of case elements as the reward signal to guide clari-
fication in legal question answering. SyLeR (Zhang et al.
2025b) addresses the lack of structured reasoning paths in
legal question answering by introducing a structure-aware
similarity-based reward mechanism that guides LLMs to
construct syllogistic reasoning paths. However, its reliance
on surface-level matching, making it difficult to capture the
complexity and rigor of legal reasoning. Simultaneously,
Legal Mathematical Reasoning (Zhang et al. 2025a) em-
ploys result-oriented rewards to enhance mathematical rea-
soning, belonging to the Reinforcement Learning with Veri-
fiable Rewards (RLVR) framework. However, this approach
falls short in effectively evaluating intermediate reason-
ing quality. Therefore, we propose Legal ∆that integrates
an information-gain-enhanced reward mechanism into the
RLVR framework to provide fine-grained supervision for
reasoning quality, enabling LLM to learn more reliable and
confidence-aligned legal reasoning strategies.

3.2 Enhancing the Reasoning Capability of LLMs
via Reinforcement Learning
General-domain LLMs often fall short in domain-
specific reasoning tasks. Although supervised fine-tuning
(SFT) (Gururangan et al. 2020) can adapt models to target
domains, it also risks overfitting and catastrophic forget-
ting (Luo et al. 2023), thereby weakening general reasoning
capabilities. To address this, we propose a RL-based
training framework. The first stage employs SFT to guide
the LLM to imitate the expert-like reasoning behaviors. The
second stage applies reinforcement learning to further refine
the reasoning ability based on performance feedback.
Supervised Fine-Tuning We construct MR1-Distill via
SFT using reasoning-enhanced legal data. Specifically, we
leverage the high-quality reasoning chains generated by
DeepSeek-R1 (Guo et al. 2025) to construct a dataset pair-
ing legal queries with structured and expert-like reasoning
steps.

over the full reasoning sequence:

3.3 Information-Gain Enhanced Legal Reward
Modeling
To move beyond terminal answer evaluation, we introduce
an information-theoretic enhancement to reward design.
Specifically, we replace the legal reward Rlegalcomponent
withRinfofor reward modeling:
R(oi) =Rformat(oi) +Rinfo(ai). (5)
The reward Rinfoincorporates reasoning confidence through
differential Q-value analysis to better evaluate the semantic
contribution of reasoning processes in legal analysis:
Rinfo(ai) =Rlegal(ai)×σ(∆Q·T), (6)
where σ(·)is the sigmoid function providing bounded am-
plification within (0,1) with monotonic enhancement proper-
ties,∆Qdenotes the information quality differential, and T
is the temperature parameter that controls the sensitivity of
reward modulation for fine-grained control over training dy-
namics. The multiplicative structure preserves the primacy
of correctness while enabling adaptive enhancement based
on information content.
Recent work by Li et al. (Li et al. 2025) proves that log-
its from next-token prediction training are mathematically
equivalent to Q-functions in offline inverse reinforcement
learning(see detailed proof in Appendix A.4):
Q(q, a) =logitθ(a|q), (7)
where Q(q, a)quantifies the expected value of generating
answer agiven query q, and logitθ(a|q)represents the av-
erage unnormalized log-probability scores across all tokens
in answer awhen generated autoregressively given context
q. This theoretical foundation enables us to extract Q-values
directly from any pre-trained language model and design a
differential mechanism to quantify reasoning quality. For an-
swera, the Q-value gain given by reasoning process ris ex-
pressed as:
∆Q(r) =logitθ(a|q, r)−logitθ(a|q). (8)
This formulation measures the semantic shift in the model’s
internal representation space induced by the reasoning pro-
cessr, providing a quantitative measure of how much the
reasoning contributes to the model’s confidence in generat-
ing the correct answer. We show our reasoning mode and
direct mode prompt templates respectively in Appendix A.6.

4 Experimental Methodology
In this section, we describe the datasets, baselines, evalua-

4.1 Datasets
In our experiments, we select training data from portions of
the CAIL2018 (Xiao et al. 2018), JEC QA (Zhong et al.
2019), and LAIC2021 (Zhong et al. 2019) datasets, which
encompass diverse legal reasoning scenarios across multi-
ple domains. We first use 450 high-quality reasoning sam-
ples distilled from DeepSeek-R1 to establish foundational
legal reasoning capabilities. Subsequently, for GRPO train-
ing of Legal ∆, we collect 6,981 samples for the training set
and 563 samples for the validation set across all tasks in our
experiments. We select knowledge-intensive legal reasoning
tasks for a comprehensive assessment, including legal article
prediction (SPP-F), criminal charge prediction (CCP), sen-
tencing prediction (SLP), similar case analysis (CAS) and
financial amount calculation (CAC), which demand sophis-
ticated reasoning over legal facts and regulations. To assess
the generalization capability of our approach, we addition-
ally evaluate performance on the out-of-domain DiscLaw
benchmark, which contains various legal professional exam-
inations distinct from our training domains. Detailed dataset
statistics can be found in the Appendix A.1.

4.3 Evaluation Metrics
We design comprehensive evaluation metrics for different
task types following Zhou et al. (2025); Yue et al. (2023).
For in-domain evaluation, we employ F1 and Jaccard (JAC)
scores for SPP-F and CCP tasks, which assess the quality
of multi-label classification. For SLP, CAS, and CAC tasks,
we use accuracy scores. For out-of-domain evaluation, we
assess generalization capability using accuracy scores across
five tasks from the DiscLaw benchmark: PAE, NJE, PFE,
UNGEE, and LBK.
4.4 Implementation Details
In our experiments, we adopt the Qwen2.5-Instruct series
due to its superior reflective capabilities and cognitively in-
formed reasoning behavior (Gandhi et al. 2025), includingthe 3B, 7B, and 14B parameter models (Qwen et al. 2025),
as backbone language models to ensure a comprehensive
evaluation across different model scales. During training, we
use a batch size of 2 per GPU and a learning rate of 5e-5. For
LoRA fine-tuning, we use a rank of 32. We use nucleus sam-
pling with temperature of 0.9 and generate G= 6 samples
per prompt. For information-gain, we set a temperature of
T= 0.2. More training implementation details of Legal ∆
are presented in Appendix A.2.

To systematically assess the contribution of each core com-

Our reinforcement learning based training strategy brings
substantial performance gains over the vanilla baseline.

reinforcement learning in enabling the legal reasoning capa-
bility of LLMs. This demonstrates that GRPO’s intra-group

Among all components, the information-gain-enhanced

performance degradation confirms the value of incorporat-

differentiation of reasoning quality. The gap between this
variant and the full Legal ∆model underscores the effective-
ness of our reward enhancement in capturing the subtle char-
acteristics of high-quality legal reasoning.

A.5 Case Study
In Table 5, we compare the performance of Legal ∆and
zero-shot models in analyzing a cross-border civil proce-

and concise arguments. This sophisticated methodology
stems from specialized training on party-specific rules and
exceptions in international litigation. The model’s question-

core legal issues, avoiding the digressive tendencies char-
